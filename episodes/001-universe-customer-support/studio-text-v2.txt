The universe was being discontinued. Mara found out while trying to return a clock.

Every morning, it skipped from seven fourteen and two seconds to seven fourteen and four.

She changed the battery. She bought another clock. Both skipped the same second.

Then a message arrived from her own number.

Please stop noticing.

Mara was an accountant. She kept receipts. She did not appreciate being threatened by a timekeeping problem.

She called the number on the warranty. A recorded voice answered.

Continuity Services. For local causality, press one. For bereavement, please contact your usual provider.

She checked the number. Correct.

To dispute your existence, remain on the line.

The hold music began.

Five piano notes. A hesitation. Then the same five notes again.

Her mother used to struggle with that passage. She would laugh, shake out her left hand, and start over. Mara had spent entire childhood evenings wishing she would learn something else.

Her mother had been dead for nine years.

There was no recording.

Continuity Services. Daniel speaking.

Where did you get that music?

It's selected from the caller's memories. Familiarity reduces distress.

Mara stood very still.

It isn't working.

I'll put that in the feedback.

She opened the warranty website on her laptop. The familiar logo appeared, then vanished.

Continuity Services. Retirement in progress.

Below it was her address.

What exactly are you retiring?

Daniel took a breath.

Your universe. Thursday, seven fourteen.

Today was Wednesday.

I'd like to speak to someone else.

Of course. The current wait is six hundred years.

Mara went to the window. A pigeon perched on the opposite roof, picking at something between its feathers.

If this is a trick, it's cruel.

May I suspend your local environment for three seconds?

You may leave me alone.

The pigeon stopped.

A falling feather hung beside it.

Mara counted. One. Two. Three.

The feather continued down.

She pulled a chair out and sat carefully, missing the seat with her first attempt.

Daniel waited.

What happens to everyone?

Residents are transferred to a supported environment. Same lives, same relationships. They won't experience the interruption.

Mara looked at the empty chair opposite her.

And people who've died?

We don't reverse deaths during a transfer.

She rubbed a ring of dried coffee on the table.

All right. When do I go?

Keys clicked.

Stopped.

Daniel?

I'm checking something.

More keys. Then a long silence.

According to your record, you aren't a resident.

I've lived here thirty-eight years.

Yes. I can see those years.

Then look at them.

I am.

His voice had lost its practiced brightness.

You're a diagnostic process. You were placed inside the environment to detect inconsistencies. Most diagnostics measure things. You appear to have developed a life around the measurements.

Mara looked at the clocks.

An accountant.

Yes.

She almost laughed.

And the missing second?

Resources being withdrawn. You noticed what the residents couldn't.

On her laptop, a new line appeared.

One maintenance process remaining.

What happens to maintenance processes?

Daniel didn't answer immediately.

They end with the environment.

Across the street, a woman opened her curtains. Mara raised a hand before remembering the woman couldn't see her through the glare.

She had never learned her name.

Transfer me.

The transfer system requires a resident record. You don't have one.

Make one.

I can't create a person in the register.

You wouldn't be creating one.

Daniel was quiet.

Mara pressed the heel of her hand against her chest. Her heart was beating much too fast for something that was only checking the clocks.

My mother. Was she real?

There was a resident at that address. A piano teacher.

Did she know what I was?

There are no records of her knowing.

Mara remembered sitting beside her on the piano stool, their legs touching. Her mother guiding one small finger onto a key.

She let herself stay there for a moment.

Then she looked at the laptop.

At the bottom of the page was a button.

Report an error.

She clicked it and read the heading.

Description of fault.

Mara.

It was the first time he'd used her name.

What happens when I submit this?

An ordinary fault comes to us.

And a fault in your rules?

He exhaled slowly.

That's outside our authority. It goes to the system's creators.

She began to type, reading the words back to herself.

A conscious person has been classified as disposable equipment.

They'll require the process itself for examination. You'll be removed from the shutdown and suspended with the report.

Mara stopped typing.

Suspended how?

No awareness. No passage of time.

Until they answer?

If they answer.

The refrigerator hummed behind her.

How long have you worked here, Daniel?

I don't know how to express it in your years.

Have they ever answered a report?

Not while I've been here.

She read her sentence again.

It looked very small in the white box.

If I close this page?

You have until tomorrow morning. I can keep the environment stable until then.

An afternoon. An evening. Enough time to walk to the bakery. Call her sister. Complain about something small, and hear someone agree.

She could spend her last hours knowing she was alive.

Or submit a claim that she was, and surrender every hour after it.

Can I add an attachment?

Yes.

She opened the laptop's recorder.

My name is Mara.

She stopped. Started again.

My name is Mara. I don't know what you'll need as evidence. I can give you my memories, but you may already have those.

Her voice was steadier now.

I'd like to hear a piece of music again. My mother never managed to play it properly. When you examine me, you'll find I could imagine a perfect version. I don't want that one.

She saved the recording.

Daniel, can you include the hold music?

Yes.

Exactly as it is.

Of course.

She attached both files.

Below the submit button was a checkbox.

I confirm that this problem has not been resolved.

For once, Mara didn't need to check her records.

She ticked it.

Will I get a reference number?

Daniel made a small sound. Almost a laugh.

Yes.

Good. I'd hate to have to explain this twice.

She pressed submit.

The refrigerator fell silent.

Outside, a feather stopped halfway to the pavement.

Mara had time to see the confirmation appear.

Then the kitchen, the clocks, and the woman sitting between them became very still.

Daniel remained on the line.

For the first time he could remember, he had nothing to say.

Somewhere beyond the systems he maintained, a telephone began to ring.
