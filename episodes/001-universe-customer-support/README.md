# Episode 001: The Universe Has a Customer Support Department

**Packaging title:** *I Called Customer Support. They Told Me My Universe Was Being Discontinued.*
**Genres:** cosmic mystery, dark comedy, existential science fiction
**Format:** third person; a narrator plus two character voices
**Target runtime:** 7–10 minutes (not yet measured from real audio)

The pilot is fully written in two versions. Neither has final publication sign-off.

## Characters

- **Mara**, 38, a practical, sceptical accountant who lives alone. Precision and receipts give her a familiar identity. Her late mother's imperfect piano playing is the story's emotional anchor.
- **Daniel**, a courteous, weary Continuity Services agent. His routine support manner slowly becomes personal concern. He's constrained by the system, not a villain. "Universal Maintenance" is background lore that the script doesn't name.

**Central question:** can a system treat an unexpectedly conscious being as disposable infrastructure? The climax is Mara's deliberate assertion of personhood at a real cost, not a rescue.

## Story

1. **Hook.** The universe is being discontinued. Mara learns this while returning a clock that skips the same second every morning. A text from her own number says: "Please stop noticing."
2. **The call.** The warranty number reaches Continuity Services. The hold music is her late mother's five-note piano passage, hesitation included; Daniel says it was chosen from her memories to reduce distress. Her laptop shows the retirement notice and her address.
3. **Stakes.** Shutdown is Thursday at seven fourteen; today is Wednesday. Daniel freezes her surroundings for three seconds as proof. Residents will be transferred with their lives and relationships intact; deaths won't be reversed.
4. **Revelation.** Mara is a diagnostic process that grew a life around its measurements, which is why she notices resources being withdrawn. Transfer needs a resident record, which Daniel can't create. Maintenance processes end with the environment.
5. **Grounding.** Her mother was a real resident and piano teacher, with no record of knowing Mara's origin. Their relationship still matters.
6. **The appeal.** A report of a fault in the rules goes past Daniel to the system's creators. Filing it means Mara is suspended for examination: no awareness, no passing time, no guaranteed answer. Daniel has never seen a reply. Or she can keep her remaining hours.
7. **Choice and ending.** Mara reports that a conscious person has been classified as equipment. She attaches a recording saying she wants to hear her mother's imperfect performance again, plus the hold music. She confirms the problem is unresolved and submits. Her world goes still; Daniel stays on the line. Somewhere beyond the systems he maintains, a telephone begins to ring.

## Continuity cautions

- The appeal is **unresolved**. Don't turn the ringing phone into confirmed salvation.
- The mother's music pays off as personal attachment, not as scientific proof of consciousness.
- The script doesn't say who sent the warning text. Don't invent an answer.
- The suspension, deletion, and transfer rules are draft story rules, not shared-universe canon. Check they're clear in listening tests.
- Older outline ideas aren't in the script and shouldn't come back automatically: premature reflections, coffee cooling before it's poured, gravity failing in 1347, Mara's observations destabilising the universe, and an ending the next morning. Earlier drafts are in Git history as source material only.
