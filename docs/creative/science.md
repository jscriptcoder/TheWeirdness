# Science in the stories

The audience should feel that *The Weirdness* understands the science it bends. A story may break a law of physics, but it shouldn't misstate the law it breaks.

## Before writing a physics-based story

Keep a short internal note with:

1. **The real principle:** two to four sentences describing the physics accurately.
2. **The departure:** in one sentence, exactly what the story changes.
3. **Consequences:** what follows logically inside the fictional model.
4. **Known limits:** where the fiction goes beyond established science.
5. **Human consequence:** the emotional or ethical dilemma the violation creates.
6. **Optional postscript:** a short, fact-checked "Science Behind the Weirdness", clearly separate from the fiction.

## Common traps

These premises can still make great fiction, as long as they're presented as counterfactuals:

- Quantum measurement does **not** require a conscious observer.
- Many-Worlds does **not** predict worlds merging.
- The Planck length is **not** established to be a literal pixel of space.
- Macroscopic time reversal doesn't follow from simply deciding entropy runs backwards.
- No scientific result is proof that we live in a simulation, or proof of any single speculative interpretation.

## Motifs the user finds appealing

Entropy and the arrow of time; superposition, decoherence, and measurement; Many-Worlds; consciousness and identity; time dilation; black holes; changing constants; the Planck scale; and the simulation hypothesis.
