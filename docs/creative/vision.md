# Vision and story rules

*The Weirdness* is a YouTube anthology of strange, immersive, audio-first stories for listeners who want to leave everyday reality for a while: before sleep, on a walk or run, or whenever they want an imaginative escape.

> Strange, immersive stories where ordinary lives collide with impossible realities: sometimes terrifying, sometimes absurd, often inspired by real physics, and always designed to leave an echo in the listener's mind.

**Audience promise:** Put on your headphones, forget the ordinary, and let an impossible story carry you somewhere unexpected.

## What the stories are

Each story blends some of:

- dark and psychological mystery;
- cosmic and existential science fiction;
- philosophical thought experiments;
- surrealism and absurd humour;
- reality glitches inspired by real quantum mechanics and laws of physics.

It is **not simply horror**. A story can be frightening, funny, melancholy, awe-inspiring, or beautiful. What unifies them is that something in familiar reality has gone subtly but profoundly wrong. The user chose the blend of dark, cosmic, philosophical, and absurd, and loves physics-inspired premises that break reality's rules; keep that central.

## Core principle: one broken law, infinite consequences

Start with an ordinary person or a recognisable situation. Change one assumption about reality. Then follow the human, emotional, practical, and philosophical consequences in a way that stays coherent inside the story.

For example:
- A household clock skips the same second every morning, because time itself is undergoing maintenance.
- People remember tomorrow but can't form memories of today.
- Observed possibilities stop resolving, and a person lives as incompatible selves.
- A mundane office processes complaints about broken physical laws.

## Five storytelling rules

1. **Start familiar.** The more ordinary the first scene, the stronger the impossibility.
2. **Lead with a simple impossible premise.** Put the listener inside the anomaly in the first seconds, before any slow setup.
3. **Treat absurdity seriously.** Characters react as believable people; institutions can discuss cosmic catastrophe in dry administrative language.
4. **Give the premise an emotional cost.** Time stories are also about regret, alternate realities about identity, cosmic stories about insignificance and meaning.
5. **Leave an echo.** End on an image, question, or revelation that makes something real feel briefly uncanny.

Vary the endings: some resolve, some reframe what came before, a few stay deliberately ambiguous. Avoid cheap twists and jump scares.

## Short format and instant hook

These rules were confirmed by the user and override any earlier, longer pacing ideas.

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Runtime** | About 10 minutes at most; 7–10 is the working target, and shorter is fine when the story earns it. Roughly 900–1,300 spoken words. Measure the real audio; don't pad. |
| **Title** | Does real storytelling work before the click: specific, strange, curiosity-provoking. Prefer a crisp impossible claim or contradiction over mood words. Never misleading. |
| **First seconds** | The most compelling premise, anomaly, or impossible line within **0–5 seconds**. No greeting, ident, subscribe request, or prologue before it. |
| **Early reward** | The first 15–30 seconds confirm the title's promise and raise a further question. |
| **Escalation** | Roughly every 30–60 seconds, a meaningful discovery, complication, choice, or shift. Not mechanical cliffhangers. Cut lines that repeat known information. |
| **One central weirdness** | One dominant broken rule or mystery. Few characters, little exposition, no tangents. |
| **Landing** | Leave time for a complete emotional or philosophical ending; never an abrupt cut. |
| **Bedtime-friendly** | Hook with an idea, not loudness. No jump scares, frantic editing, or aggressive music. |

Three quick tests:
- **Title:** seeing only the title in a feed, does a listener sense an impossible event and need an answer?
- **Opening:** after five seconds, is the promised weirdness already happening?
- **Retention:** if a paragraph adds no meaning, tension, or texture, cut it.

## Listening first

- Dialogue, narration, and sound carry all essential plot information. The listener never needs to look at the screen.
- Keep sound calm and loudness controlled for bedtime. No loud stingers.
- Keep narrative momentum and curiosity for walkers and runners.
- Use clean, distinct voices and easy-to-follow scenes. Avoid many proper nouns or complicated timelines without orientation.
- Use silence on purpose.
- Visuals support mood and discoverability but never carry essential information.
